# MedLuxLife – translation rules and glossary (German de_DE, Turkish tr_TR)

Source language: British English. Translate ONLY the given strings; each string is one text node of a live page.

## Style rules (both languages)
- Translate the meaning, not word for word. Natural, fluent, grammatically perfect language, as a high-quality local clinic or premium travel agency would write it. Short, clear sentences. No literal calques.
- Formal address: German "Sie" (Ihr/Ihnen, capitalised); Turkish "siz" (formal, -iniz/-ınız, polite imperative "-in/-ın", e.g. "Bize ulaşın").
- Calm, warm, trustworthy, premium tone. No exclamation marks unless the English has one.
- Medical advertising law (German HWG, also apply to Turkish): no promises or guarantees of results, no superlatives that are not in the source, no "painless/risk-free/permanent" claims added. Keep cautious words from the source ("usually" = "in der Regel" / "genellikle", "may" = "kann" / "-ebilir").
- Keep facts exactly: numbers, ranges ("2–4" keeps the en dash), units, time spans, ages, percentages.
- Keep unchanged: MedLuxLife, WhatsApp, email addresses, phone numbers, URLs, brand-like technique names (FUE, DHI, PRP, NAD+, Choi, Vaser, SMAS, All-on-4, All-on-6, BBL, IVF, MRI/CT where an abbreviation), "Learn more →" arrow characters (→), numbering like "1." at the start.
- HTML entities stay as entities: "&amp;" stays "&amp;", "&#8217;" may become the language's normal apostrophe/character.
- Capitalisation: keep the role of the string – a heading stays a heading (German: nouns capitalised as always; Turkish: sentence case for long headings, title case only where the English uses short title-style menu items), a button stays a short call to action, a list item stays a list item (no added full stop if the English has none).
- Never name the partner clinic or hospital or any doctor; "our partner hospital" = "unser Partnerkrankenhaus" / "partner hastanemiz"; "our partner clinic" = "unsere Partnerklinik" / "partner kliniğimiz".
- Medical documents: never ask people to send documents to MedLuxLife; if the English says documents go directly to the doctor, keep that meaning exactly.
- German: use "ß" correctly, real German typographic quotes „…“ only if the source has quotes, compound nouns correctly joined (Bauchdeckenstraffung, Haartransplantation). Gender: generic forms are fine ("Ihr Chirurg", "Ärztinnen und Ärzte" where the source speaks of doctors in general).
- Turkish: correct use of İ/ı, ş, ğ, ç, ö, ü; correct vowel harmony and suffixes after abbreviations and foreign words (e.g. "FUE'de", "DHI'da", "MedLuxLife'ın", "WhatsApp üzerinden"); "Türkiye" (never "Türkei"/"Turkey"); "İstanbul" with dotted capital İ.
- The English uses "Türkiye": German = "Türkei", Turkish = "Türkiye".
