Who it may be suitable for

  • Your upper eyelids droop or you have bags under the eyes.
  • Excess skin may even narrow your field of vision.
  • You are in good general health and do not smoke, or are ready to stop for the surgery.

Whether the procedure is suitable for you is decided by the surgeon after a personal assessment.

Options

Upper eyelids

Removes excess skin from the upper lids; usually under local anaesthesia.

Lower eyelids

Treats bags under the eyes; generally under general anaesthesia.

How the operation is performed

Consultation with the surgeon

Before the operation, the surgeon examines you in person, discusses your wishes and confirms the final plan.

Anaesthesia and preparation

The type of anaesthesia – local or general anaesthesia – is planned with you in advance. The team marks and prepares the area to be treated.

The procedure

The surgeon removes excess skin and, where needed, fat through fine incisions in the natural folds of the eyelids.

Closure and first care

The incisions are closed with fine stitches and protected with tapes. Your surgeon tells you whether you go home the same day.

Recovery step by step

First days

Swelling and bruising are normal. Most people resume light routines after 3–5 days.

Day 7

The stitches and tapes are removed by the surgeon after about 7 days.

Weeks 1–2

Most people are back to their normal routine after 1–2 weeks; swelling continues to go down.
